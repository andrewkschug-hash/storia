# La Casa delle Finestre (The House of Windows) (la-casa-delle-finestre)

## Chapter 1: The grid (La griglia)

Irene arrives in San Quirico sul Nera in the afternoon. The town is small. Irene looks for Via della Scuola on foot. The suitcase is heavy, but she walks. The street is narrow and there is little noise. Loredana is standing in front of the door of the house.

Good evening. Are you Irene Colombo? Yes. I’ve just arrived. Good. This is the house. You’re upstairs, in the attic. The stairs are on the left. I live on the floor below. If you need something, knock. The street is quiet, in the day and in the evening.

Irene goes up the stairs with the suitcase. She opens the door of the attic. The room is small, but there is a large window onto the street. There is a bed, a table, and a chair. Irene leaves the suitcase near the bed. Then she opens the window and looks out.

Opposite there is a large building. It has many windows. One above the other, one next to the other. It looks like a grid. What is that house opposite? It’s the middle school. It has been closed for two years. Nobody goes in. In the daytime the windows are dark. Irene looks at the school for a minute. She does not see anyone at the windows. Then she closes the window because it is chilly.

When it is evening, Irene is alone in the attic. She turns on a light in the house and eats something. Then she goes back to the window. Outside it was dark. The school looked empty. Nobody was walking in the street. Irene stays there and looks. There is no car. There is no voice.

While she is looking, she sees a light. There is a light in a window, on the second floor. It is not strong. But it is there. Irene has seen the light and she does not move. She waits a minute, then two. The light goes out. Then it comes on in another window. It is not the same window. It is on the same row.

Someone? Irene does not see anyone in the street. So she takes a notebook and a pen. She writes: first one window, then another. She writes the time as well. It is late. She waits again. Then she sees a third light. Three windows. Irene looks again. The three windows are on the same horizontal line. It is not a line up and down. It is a line to the right and to the left. She does not understand. But she writes that down too.

## Chapter 2: The closed school (La scuola chiusa)

This morning Irene opened the window. She looked at the school opposite for a minute. In the daytime the school looks empty. The doors are shut. The windows on the row are dark. Irene picked up the notebook from the table. Last night there was a light, then another, then a third. This morning there is no light. But she wrote down the time. So it is not a dream. The school used to be a school. Now it looks like only a closed building.

Irene goes down the stairs. Loredana is in the kitchen with the coffee. Good morning. Did you sleep? Yes, thank you. But I have a question. Is the school really empty? Loredana does not look at Irene. She speaks quickly, like a lesson. There was an earthquake. Two years ago. The school is not safe. You do not go in. Nobody goes in. Everyone says that. Irene listens and almost believes it. In the daytime this story seems true.

Irene goes out into the street. The sun is already high. She walks as far as the school. A woman goes by with a bag. Excuse me, the school is closed, right? The woman answers quickly. It’s empty. You do not go in. Nobody goes in. Then the woman walks away. The words are the same as Loredana’s. Irene stays a moment. Then she goes to the big door. The door is shut. Irene does not try to open it. The courtyard gate is shut as well.

There is a courtyard behind a gate. Irene looks at the courtyard. There is a man with grey hair. He has water in a can. He waters the plants by the wall. But the plants are dead. They are yellow and dry. The water falls on the dry earth and does no good. The man waters them again. If the school is empty, why the water? Irene almost still believes Loredana. But the man is there, in the daytime. The water is real. The dead plants are real.

Loredana is at the window downstairs. That’s Quinto. He was the caretaker. Irene does not speak to him. She looks up, toward last night’s row. Irene counts three dark windows. The three windows are dark. There is no light. Quinto raises his head. He looks at the same windows. He does not look at Irene. Then he takes the can and walks away. He says nothing to Irene.

## Chapter 3: The contract (Il contratto)

The next day Irene goes to the town hall. The town hall is in the square, not far from the school. This morning the school windows are dark again. Irene does not stop. She walks to the door of the town hall and goes in. She has a short contract. Today is the first day. From nine she has to be here.

The office is small and quiet. There is a woman behind a desk. She smiles. Good morning, Dottoressa Alme. Good morning, Irene. Please sit down. Irene sits down. She signs the contract on the desk. Alme keeps one copy. Irene keeps the other copy of the contract. I am the director, for these papers. The contract is for two months. Then we’ll see. You scan the old papers of the town hall. Including what is left from the school. Nothing important. Whatever is left, in short. Alme smiles again. Irene listens and says little. That’s fine. I work with papers. Perfect. This is her job, then.

The boxes are downstairs. There is also a scanner, on a table. Irene goes down with Alme. There is dust and there is little light. There are many folders and many boxes. Alme points to a box near the scanner. Start from here. School papers, old years. You scan them, that’s all. You do not have to read everything. Alme goes back upstairs, still with the smile. Irene stays alone with the boxes.

Irene opens the first folder. There are lists and simple sheets. Dates, classes, nothing odd. She puts the sheet on the scanner. She has scanned three folders. The work is slow, but it is clear. Almost an hour goes by. Then she sees another box, further back. It is not Alme’s box. There is a folder on top of the others. On the cover there is a line in black pen. Do not scan. Irene reads it twice. Her hand is on the folder. Should I open this folder? Should I ask Alme now? But Alme said: you scan them, that’s all. Irene does not open the folder. She puts it aside, closed. She takes another folder, an ordinary one. She scans again for twenty minutes. The ordinary papers are easy. The closed folder stays by the wall. Irene has finished three small boxes, not all of them.

When Irene comes back up, Alme is at the door. She smiles. All well downstairs? Yes. I saw a folder. It says: do not scan. Alme does not lose her smile. That one stays there. It is not for today. If you find anything odd, you come to me. Not to the others. At midday you may leave. Irene says yes. But she thinks of the closed folder, downstairs. She has not opened it.

## Chapter 4: The notebook (Il quaderno)

After work Irene is in the attic. The school opposite is closed, as in the daytime. Irene opens the notebook on the table. She does not want a story. She wants the numbers, as on the folders. She takes a pen and looks at the windows. She counts. Five windows per floor, opposite her. She draws the school: many squares, a grid. It is not beautiful. It is clear. Then she puts on the numbers. Second floor, from the left: one, two, three, four, five. Third floor: six, seven, eight, nine, ten. Monday night’s row is three, four, five. Irene knows this because on Monday she saw that line. She writes Monday at the top of the page. Irene writes the three numbers too.

When it is evening, Irene turns off the light in the room. She leaves only a small light on the table, for the notebook. She sits near the window. She waits. Outside the school looks empty again. Nobody is walking in the street. Irene looks at the clock. It is five to nine. She waits again. Then it is nine o’clock.

While she is looking, she sees a light. It is window three, on the second floor. Like Monday, Irene thinks. Irene writes: Tuesday, nine, window three. She waits. Then the light goes out. After two minutes another window comes on. It is window four. Still the same row. Irene writes: nine-oh-two, window four. Then, again, dark. Irene waits for window five. She looks at the row. Five stays dark. She waits a minute, then another. At nine ten there is a third light. Irene looks at the drawing. It is not window five. It is window eight, on the third floor. She writes: nine ten, window eight.

The light stays a little, then it goes out. Irene opens Monday’s page. Monday: first three, then four, then five. The whole row, the same floor. Tuesday: first three, then four, then eight. The order is not the same. The line is not the same. Irene compares the two drawings. First, then, after: the numbers do not match. On Monday window eight was dark. No light, the whole evening. On Tuesday the last light is right there. Irene does not go out. Irene does not understand why. But she writes the time, the number, the floor. She reads the times again: nine, nine-oh-two, nine ten. The third floor, on Monday, was all dark. On Tuesday, no. She closes the notebook. She does not have an answer. She only has two pages, with the numbers. Irene leaves the pen next to the closed notebook. Window eight stays on Tuesday’s page. The school is dark again.

## Chapter 5: Mimmo (Mimmo)

In the afternoon Irene comes back from the square. Near the school there are three young people. They have a phone and they are laughing. One is older than the others. He is nineteen, or so he says. He has a red jacket and talks loudly. Irene walks slowly, because she hears the words. The boy in the jacket talks to his friends. He says there is a ghost. In the school. At the windows. Irene listens because she too has seen a light. For a moment the kids’ story seems possible.

Irene stops. She almost believes it, for a moment. She has seen the lights too. Excuse me. What have you seen? The boy in the jacket smiles. A ghost. In the empty school. A light. I filmed it. My name is Mimmo. I film the school for fun. Look. The two friends laugh again. Irene does not laugh.

Mimmo gives her the phone. Irene watches the video. She watches it twice. There is the school, in daytime, a little dark. Then a light in a window, on the second floor. The light moves and then that’s all. The video is short. Irene almost believes it again. It looks like the same window as Monday. When did you make it? At night, of course. The ghost is there at night. The kids say yes.

Irene does not argue. She looks down, on the phone. There is the time of the video. There is also the day, small, under the picture. Irene reads it calmly. It is not night. It is afternoon. Ten past four. Irene shows the time to Mimmo. Mimmo looks and shrugs. The phone is wrong. Or it’s the ghost in the daytime. Better. The friends laugh. Irene gives the phone back. Thank you. She says nothing else. But now she knows one thing. That video is not the evening light. It is an afternoon game. Irene looks at the school now, at twenty past four. The windows are dark. No light, no video.

Irene goes home. She goes up to the attic and waits. When it is almost evening, she looks at the street again. Mimmo is not there. The kids are not there. The phone, the laughter, the ghost: nothing. Irene opens the notebook. She writes: Mimmo video, afternoon, ten past four. She waits at the window. Outside it grows dark. It is eight, then half past eight. The street is empty. Then, at nine, there is a light. Window three, second floor. It is not a video. It is not afternoon. Mimmo has gone. The light, though, is there all the same. Irene writes down the time. She does not write the word ghost. She writes only: nine, window three, like Monday.

## Chapter 6: Quinto (Quinto)

The next day, in the afternoon, Irene goes past the school. The courtyard gate is shut, as always. Quinto is there, inside. Irene is outside, in the street. Between them there is the gate. Today he does not have the watering can. He is not watering. He stands by the wall, with his hands in his pockets. The dead plants are still there. Irene stops. Good morning. You are Quinto, right? Quinto looks up. Yes. The caretaker. I still am, a little. For many years. Irene stays on the street side. She does not try the gate.

Irene does not ask many questions. It’s a nice day. It is not cold. Yes. There’s sun. There is little wind today. I live opposite, in the attic. Loredana told me. You work at the town hall, don’t you? Yes. A short contract. Quinto nods. The town is small. Everyone knows everything. Then he is silent. Today there is no water for the plants. Or he does not want to water them. You’re not watering today, Signor Quinto? Not today. That’s enough. Irene does not ask why.

Quinto takes his hands out of his pockets. There is a bunch of keys. Many keys, on a ring. Quinto counts them quickly, in a low voice. One, two, three, four… then he stops. Some are small. One is large. Irene looks at the keys, not at Quinto’s face. Do those keys open the school? Quinto holds the bunch in his hand. He points to the large key. This is the door. Wait. Then he moves his finger. He points to another key, smaller. No. This one. Then he changes again. The big door is this other one. The gate… this one, maybe. Irene listens. Three keys, three answers. She does not understand which key is the real one. Quinto puts the bunch back in his pocket quickly. The keys make a noise. Irene hears that noise.

And the upper floor? Quinto shakes his head. I don’t go upstairs. The classrooms, the corridors: no. Never. I don’t go. It isn’t my floor. There is no light, there is no need. The school has been closed for two years. Irene looks up, toward windows three, four, five. And eight, on the third floor. Today, in the daytime, they are dark. Quinto does not follow her gaze. He looks at the courtyard, or the wall, or nothing.

Irene says little. All right. Thank you. Goodbye. Quinto is already turning away. Then he speaks, toward the gate, not toward Irene. The school is empty. He does not look at her when he says it. Irene walks toward home. At home she opens the notebook. She writes: keys. Not upstairs. The school is empty? She closes the notebook.

## Chapter 7: The timetable (L'orario)

At the town hall Irene goes down to the boxes again. Today she works on the allowed papers, not on the other folder. The closed folder stays by the wall. Irene does not touch it. She opens one of Alme’s boxes and scans. Lists, dates, classes. Nothing odd. Then, at the back, she finds a different sheet. It is a photocopy, a little faint. Irene found it among old papers, not in the forbidden folder. At the top there is a word: timetable. Underneath: middle school, last year. Irene has read it twice. So she puts the sheet aside, for later. When Alme goes by in the corridor, Irene scans another list. Alme said: you do not have to read everything. But this is a timetable. It is a school paper.

At midday Irene leaves. She has put the photocopy among her papers, calmly. It is an old timetable, in an open box. At home she opens the notebook and the photocopy on the same table. She puts the photocopy on the left, the notebook on the right. The table is small. The two pages fit. When she looks at the timetable, she sees classes and hours. Monday, nine o’clock: class 1A, room two. Then, after that, class 1B, room three. Then class 2A, room four. Tuesday, nine o’clock: again 1A, room two. Then 1B, room three. Then, though, class 3C, room eight, third floor.

Irene takes the pen. While she looks at the drawing of the grid, she puts the room numbers. Room two: window three, second floor. Room three: window four. Room four: window five. Room eight: window eight, third floor. Then she opens the page of the lights. She looks at one page, then the other. Monday, on the timetable: room two, three, four. Monday, on the lights: three, then four, then five. Tuesday, on the timetable: room two, three, eight. Tuesday, on the lights: three, then four, then eight. The two pages coincide. Irene writes the numbers one under the other, without a sentence. Irene still does not know why. She does not know who carries the light. No name on the timetable, only classes and rooms.

Irene reads the sheet once more. In one cell there is a circle, in old pen. It is not her pen. The circle is on room two, Monday, nine o’clock. Irene looks at the drawing. Room two is window three. Window three is opposite the attic. Irene goes to the window and looks. Yes. That room is right opposite. Someone circled that room, years ago. Irene does not understand why. She only has a timetable, and the lights that return. She closes the notebook on top of the photocopy.

## Chapter 8: She is thinking of me (Lei pensa a me)

The two pages coincide. Irene still knows that. But the pages do not say whether someone sees her. In the evening she stands at the window, on purpose. Window three is opposite. It is room two, on the sheet. The notebook stays closed on the table, for now. Tonight she does not count first. She only looks. She has turned off the light in the room. Outside it is dark. Nobody is walking in the street. The school looks empty. Irene waits for nine o’clock. It is five to nine. Then nine. On the pages, at this hour, the first light was already there.

While she is looking, she sees a light. Window four, then window five. On the pages, Monday was three, four, five. Tonight the light does not start from three. Irene does not move. Then the light is at window three. Opposite. Irene waits for it to go on, as on the pages. The light does not go on. It stops. Irene remembers Monday’s numbers: three, four, five. She remembers Tuesday: three, four, eight. Tonight: four, five, then three. Then nothing. This pause is not on the sheet. The pages coincide on the rooms. Not on this waiting. Irene holds the curtain with one hand.

A minute. Two. Three, maybe. Irene counts slowly, without a voice. The light stays. It seems that someone is there. It seems that they are looking toward the attic. Maybe. Maybe not. Maybe the light is only still. If someone is there, they see me, Irene thinks. If nobody is there, it is only a light. The two pages do not answer. Irene takes a step back. Then another. She does not turn on her light. She does not knock on the glass. But she stays near the window. Her heart goes fast. Her hands almost do not. Nobody calls. Nobody speaks.

Then, after a long time, the light goes out. It does not go on. It does not come back. Window three is dark. Irene waits another five minutes. The light does not come back. Opposite, nothing. Did someone see her? She does not know. Irene does not know if the pause was for her. She opens the notebook. She writes: window three, it stops, then dark. She also writes: it is not on the pages. Then she writes: maybe for me. Then she erases the last three words. She leaves the pause. She does not write the why.

## Chapter 9: Yesterday (Ieri)

This morning Irene opened the notebook. The two pages still coincide: the rooms, the numbers. Yesterday the light did not follow those pages. It did four, five, then three. Then it stopped. She wrote it: it stops, then dark. It is not on the sheet. She erased maybe for me. That line is gone. The pause remains. A problem remains, not an answer. Usually, at nine, there is a light. Yesterday there was one, and it stopped. Irene writes a new line: tonight I check again. She puts the two timetable pages under this page. First they coincide. Then there is yesterday. Two facts, one under the other. She does not write someone. She does not write why. Then she went to the town hall, like every day.

Work was short, but she did the work. She scanned lists. Dates, names, nothing odd. The closed folder was still there, by the wall. Irene looked at it. She did not open it. Yesterday there was a pause. Today there is a folder. It is not the same thing. Alme smiled and said little. Irene did not mention the pause. She did not mention yesterday. Going home, she goes past the school in daylight. The windows are empty. There is no light. It is not needed, in the day. It looks empty, as everyone says.

When it is evening, Irene is at the window again, on purpose. She turned off the room light, like yesterday. She waits for nine. Then nine ten. Then nine twenty. If the pause was for her, tonight someone comes back. Or not. Irene does not choose. She only looks. Tonight there is no light. The school stays dark. All the windows are dark. Usually there is a light. Yesterday it stopped. Tonight nothing. So yesterday was not a timetable. Tonight is not a timetable. Irene waits another ten minutes. She counts, slowly, without a voice. Nobody walks. No light. No pause. She opens the notebook. She writes: tonight nothing. She does not write maybe. She does not erase yesterday. So she goes to bed late, with two different evenings in her head. If she was wrong yesterday, tonight shows it. If she was not wrong, tonight is another fact.

The morning after, Irene goes past the school, early. Near the gate there is something on the ground, in the gutter. It is a pack. Inside there were camping batteries. Now the pack is empty, or almost. Irene picks it up with two fingers. It is not old. There is no dust. There is no dry rain. There is a small price, and a recent date. Someone bought batteries this week. Irene looks at the windows. By day they are empty. At night, yesterday, there was a light. In the closed school the ordinary lights do not come on. So that light was not the school. It was a camping light, with batteries. It is not a video. It is not a ghost. It is a thing you buy. Last night there was no light. But the batteries are here. Irene puts the pack in her pocket. She does not go in. She does not call anyone. She looks at the shut gate, then goes to work. Mimmo has a video in the day. This pack is for the night. Two different stories. Irene keeps this one, in her pocket.

## Chapter 10: Do not scan (Non scansionare)

Today Irene is alone downstairs, with the boxes. Alme is upstairs, in the office. The scanner is on. First she does the allowed work. It is her job. She opens three ordinary folders. Inside there are lists, dates, stamps. She scans one page, then another. She counts. She writes the numbers on the town-hall sheet. Nothing odd. Papers that should stay together. She puts the three folders back in order, with the right numbers. The town hall wants a clean archive. Irene knows how to do this. Then she looks at the closed folder, by the wall. The tape is different. The paper is different. The line is still there: do not scan. The other folders have the same stamp. This one does not. The town-hall stamp is missing. The list number is missing. Someone kept it out of the ordinary work. I should leave it, she thinks. But this morning there were the batteries. I should tell Alme. But Alme said: you come to me. Irene waits a minute. Nobody comes down the stairs. Irene opens the folder.

Inside there is a report, not long. Three pages. At the top there is a name: Sara Neri, teacher. She wrote about cracks in the school, before the closure. The wall is not safe, the report says. The children, no. She also writes: before the closure, not after. In the town-hall lists the school has been closed for two years. This report is from before. Someone closed, and put the paper away. Irene reads the first page, then the second, slowly. There is a date, two years ago, or a little more. There is a signature. In the other boxes the school is closed, and that is all. Here the school is not only closed. Here there is a wall, and a fear. This paper is not with the others. It has not been scanned. Someone put it aside, on purpose. It is not right, Irene thinks. But it is only a folder. She opens the notebook. She copies the name. She copies: cracks. She copies the date. She also copies: do not scan. Not as an order. As a fact. She does not copy everything. She is not a lawyer. She is an archivist. She does not put the report on the scanner. She closes it. She leaves it where it was. She looks for Sara Neri in the other boxes, quickly, calmly. Lists. She does not find other photos of her. She does not find other reports. So it is not Sara’s archive. It is a hidden paper.

When she looks up, Alme is at the door. She smiles. She did not say her name, downstairs. How many folders, today? Three. The ordinary ones. She does not count the fourth. It is not in the three. It is not on the town-hall sheet. All well? Yes. Did you find anything odd? Alme speaks slowly, carefully. She looks at the boxes, not at the closed folder. Irene looks at the folder, then at Alme. No. It is the first time she says no, like this. A lie, small, clear. I should say it, she thinks. But she said no. Alme does not lose her smile. Good. Then upstairs, a coffee. Irene goes up. She drinks the coffee. The name stays downstairs, in the folder, and in the notebook. Alme talks about the contract, the days, the scanner. If there is anything odd, you come to me. She has already said it. She says it again. Irene says yes. She has already said no, downstairs. After the coffee Irene goes down again, for a minute. The folder is closed, as before. The tape is in its place. Nobody must see that she opened it. It is an archivist’s thought, and a liar’s. She goes back up. Alme is still in the office. She does not ask why Irene went down.

## Chapter 11: Sara (Sara)

In the afternoon Irene opens another allowed box. There are class photos, old years, names underneath. One photo is of the last class, two years ago, or a little more. Behind the students there is a teacher. She is not in the middle. She is to the side, near a window. Irene looks at the face for a long time. Then the window in the photo. She does not know which room it is. She only knows: a window, a school, a teacher. That window is not her attic. It is over there, in a classroom. Under the photo there is a small name: Sara Neri. She is the same person as in the folder. Almost the same date. Irene compares, in her head: date of the report, date of the photo. Close. First the paper. Then the class. Then away. Irene does not speak. She holds the photo with two hands. She looks at the other photos in the box. Earlier years, other faces, other classes. Sara Neri is only there at the end. Last class, last window. She was not always here. Then she was. Then they sent her away.

A man from the office goes by and sees the photo. It is not Alme. Ah, Neri. They said she exaggerated. She wrote. She stayed after the children. Then they sent her away. They also said she took papers home, the man says, already far off. Irene does not know if it is true. She keeps the sentence, like the other one. Irene looks up. Away, why? Irene hears her own voice: too much. Then it is too late. Because she exaggerated, the man says. The school was the school. That’s all. Then he takes a coffee and goes. Irene does not ask where she lives now. She does not ask about the light. She keeps three things: she wrote, she stayed, she exaggerated. Writing was her job, Irene thinks. Staying after the children, too. Exaggerated is the town’s word. It is not a date. It is not a crack. The report is there. The photo is there. The town’s sentence is there. She puts the photo in the box, calmly. She does not scan it. She writes in the notebook: teacher, last class, window in the photo. She does not write: she is the light. She does not know.

In the evening Irene walks home. She is not looking for Sara. She is going home. The stop is there, on the way. There is a bus stop, near the square, before the school. Irene stops because the light is red, not because she is looking. Then she sees the woman. She was already there, or she arrives now: Irene does not know. A woman is waiting. She has a large, stiff bag. Irene looks at the face, from the side, as in the photo. She looks like the woman in the photo. Maybe. The age, the hair, the side. Maybe not. It is evening, and there is little light. The woman does not look at Irene. She looks at the closed school, a moment, then at the road. It is not a signal. It is a look. Irene does not answer, because there is nothing to answer. The bus arrives. The woman gets on. She does not run. She does not speak. The bag looks heavy. Like paper, not like shopping. If she took papers home then, maybe she carries papers now. Maybe not. Irene is not sure. She does not say: it is her. She does not say: it is not her. She does not take the bus. She does not call. She watches the bus leave. At home she writes: maybe Sara. Then she adds: I don’t know. She also adds: bag like paper. She looks at the school, not at me. It is not proof. It is an evening. Irene knows that. She closes the notebook. She does not ask Loredana. Tomorrow there is the town hall again. Tomorrow the photo is in the box again. That she wrote was true: the report is there. That she stayed, that she exaggerated: this she does not know.

## Chapter 12: The second floor (Il secondo piano)

In the evening Irene does not go up to the attic at once. From the attic she only sees the facing grid: window three, room two. Tonight she wants to see more. She stays in the street, on this side, in front of the school. She has the notebook. She has the drawing of the grid. She holds it low, under the streetlight. She puts the drawing next to the building. The numbers and the windows must coincide from this side too. From here the grid is wider. From the attic you only see a piece. Irene moves the sheet. She looks for three. She finds it, opposite her house. She does not try the gate. She does not go in. She is on this side. The school is over there. She counts the floors: ground floor, first, second. She also counts the second-floor windows, from the left. Three is opposite her, at home. Four and five are further along. Tonight’s light is not on three. It is further, toward the right. On the drawing, on the second floor to the right, the teachers’ room is there, or so it seems. Window three is further left. Opposite the attic. At nine there is a light, on the second floor, to the right. It is not window three. It is not yesterday’s pause. It is another room, over there. So the light does not stay only where Irene can see it from home. Irene writes: second floor, teachers’ room, maybe. Not window three. She does not go up. She does not call. She only looks, from this side. From the attic, yesterday, the light was at three, opposite. From the street, tonight, the light is on the second floor, elsewhere. Two points of view. Two rooms. The person, if there is one, moves.

Then a second light moves, behind her. For a moment she thinks: it answers. Someone is coming. Then she understands. It is not the school. It is her house, on the stairs, on this side. Irene turns. Her heart goes fast. Someone is coming down, on this side, with a small light. It is not a camping torch. It is a phone, on. Loredana is in a dressing gown. Her hair is messy. Irene. You’re back late. Work, Irene says. She does not say: the school. Sorry. I didn’t hear. I was looking over there. Loredana lowers the phone. She does not look at the school. I check the street. Then the engine starts, and she goes away from this small town.
