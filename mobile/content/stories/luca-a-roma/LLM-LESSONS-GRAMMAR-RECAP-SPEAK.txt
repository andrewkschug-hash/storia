GRAMMAR BATCH 1–5: Essere and avere
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 1-5
Title: Essere and avere

Intro:
Chapters 1-5 lean on two verbs: essere (to be) and avere (to have). Here is exactly how they work in the sentences you read.

### Step 1: Essere tells you who someone is, where they are, or how they feel

Explanation:
Essere is "to be." In Italian you change the ending of the verb to match the subject - but you often leave out io / tu / lui because the ending already tells you who is speaking.

- è = he / she / it is (Luca è stanco -> Luca is tired)
- sono = I am (Sono a Roma -> I am in Rome)
- sei = you are (informal)

When you see è after a name, read it as "is": Sofia è al bar = Sofia is at the bar.

Rule: Name + è + adjective/place = "[Name] is ..."

Examples:
  IT: Luca è a Roma.
  EN: Luca is in Rome.
  IT: Sofia è stanca.
  EN: Sofia is tired.
  IT: È mattina.
  EN: It is morning.

### Step 2: Avere tells you what someone has - including feelings

Explanation:
Avere is "to have." Italian uses it for possession and for many feelings English expresses with "to be."

- ho = I have (Ho fame -> literally "I have hunger" = I am hungry)
- ha = he / she has (Luca ha una casa -> Luca has a house)
- hai = you have

If an English sentence says "I am hungry / thirsty / sleepy," Italian usually says "I have hunger / thirst / sleep."

Rule: Ho / ha + noun often = "I am / he is ..." in English

Examples:
  IT: Ho fame.
  EN: I am hungry. (lit. I have hunger)
  IT: Luca ha poco soldi.
  EN: Luca has little money.
  IT: Non ho molti soldi.
  EN: I do not have much money.

### Step 3: Non goes right before the verb to make a sentence negative

Explanation:
To say "not," put non immediately before the verb. Nothing else moves.

Affirmative: Ho fame. -> Negative: Non ho fame.
Affirmative: Luca è stanco. -> Negative: Luca non è stanco.

Non is one word - do not split it from the verb.

Rule: Non + verb = negative sentence

Examples:
  IT: Non ho fame.
  EN: I am not hungry.
  IT: Luca non è a casa.
  EN: Luca is not at home.
  IT: Non è tardi.
  EN: It is not late.

### Practice

Q1. Luca is tired. Which sentence is correct?
     [0] Luca ha stanco.
   → [1] Luca è stanco.
     [2] Luca sono stanco.
   Explanation: Use essere (è) for "is" + adjective: Luca è stanco. Avere (ha) is for possession, not adjectives like "tired."

Q2. How do you say "I am hungry" in Italian?
     [0] Sono fame.
   → [1] Ho fame.
     [2] Ho affamato.
   Explanation: Italian says "I have hunger": Ho fame. You need avere (ho), not essere, for this feeling.

Q3. Which sentence means "Luca does not have much money"?
   → [0] Luca non ha molti soldi.
     [1] Luca ha non molti soldi.
     [2] Non Luca ha molti soldi.
   Explanation: Non goes directly before the verb: non ha.

GRAMMAR BATCH 6–10: Volere and cercare
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 6-10
Title: Volere and cercare

Intro:
Luca starts making plans in Rome. Two verbs drive that: volere (to want) and cercare (to look for). Here is how to build those sentences.

### Step 1: Volere: voglio / vuole + another verb

Explanation:
Volere means "to want." To say what you want to do, use volere + an infinitive (the -are / -ere / -ire form):

• voglio = I want → Voglio mangiare. (I want to eat.)
• vuole = he / she wants → Luca vuole un lavoro. (Luca wants a job.)
• vuoi = you want

You can also want a thing (noun): vuole un caffè = he wants a coffee.

Rule: Volere + infinitive = "want to ..."

Examples:
  IT: Voglio mangiare.
  EN: I want to eat.
  IT: Luca vuole un lavoro.
  EN: Luca wants a job.
  IT: Vuole restare a Roma.
  EN: He wants to stay in Rome.

### Step 2: Cercare: a regular verb for "to look for"

Explanation:
Cercare ends in -are, so it follows the same pattern as parlare or mangiare:

• cerco = I look for / I am looking for
• cerchi = you look for
• cerca = he / she looks for

Put what you are searching for right after the verb: Cerco un lavoro. Word order is flexible — Cerco un lavoro al caffè and Al caffè cerco un lavoro both work.

Rule: Cerco / cerca + thing = "I am / he is looking for ..."

Examples:
  IT: Luca cerca un lavoro.
  EN: Luca is looking for a job.
  IT: Cerchi un lavoro?
  EN: Are you looking for a job?
  IT: Scusa, cerco un lavoro.
  EN: Excuse me, I am looking for a job.

### Step 3: Scusa and per favore soften requests

Explanation:
Scusa (excuse me — informal) and per favore (please) do not change grammar, but they appear constantly when Luca asks strangers for help.

Scusa often opens a question: Scusa, cerco un lavoro.
Per favore follows a request: Un caffè, per favore.

Rule: Scusa + question = polite opening

Examples:
  IT: Scusa, dov'è il bar?
  EN: Excuse me, where is the bar?
  IT: Un caffè, per favore.
  EN: A coffee, please.
  IT: Scusa, cerco un lavoro.
  EN: Excuse me, I am looking for a job.

### Practice

Q1. "I want to eat." Which is correct?
   → [0] Voglio mangiare.
     [1] Voglio mangio.
     [2] Mangio voglio.
   Explanation: After volere, use the infinitive mangiare — not the conjugated mangio.

Q2. Luca is looking for a job. Pick the right sentence.
   → [0] Luca cerca un lavoro.
     [1] Luca cerco un lavoro.
     [2] Luca cerca lavoro un.
   Explanation: Third person uses cerca: Luca cerca un lavoro.

Q3. Which sentence politely starts a question to a stranger?
   → [0] Scusa, dov'è il bar?
     [1] Dov'è scusa il bar?
     [2] Scusa il bar dov'è?
   Explanation: Scusa comes at the start: Scusa, dov'è il bar?

GRAMMAR BATCH 11–15: Prepositions: a, in, con, per
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 11-15
Title: Prepositions: a, in, con, per

Intro:
Small words — a, in, con, per — tell you direction, location, company, and purpose. Here is what each one actually does.

### Step 1: A = to / at (cities and people)

Explanation:
With cities, Italian often uses a for both movement and location:

• Luca va a Roma. = Luca goes to Rome. (movement)
• Marco è a Roma. = Marco is in Rome. (location)
• a casa = home / at home
• a Marco = to Marco (giving something to someone)

The verb tells you which reading: andare/venire/tornare → usually "to"; essere/stare → usually "in/at".

Combined with the: a + il = al, a + la = alla. Luca va al caffè = Luca goes to the café.

Rule: a + city: "to" with movement verbs, "in/at" with essere

Examples:
  IT: Luca va a Roma.
  EN: Luca goes to Rome.
  IT: Vado al caffè.
  EN: I am going to the café.
  IT: Marco è a Roma.
  EN: Marco is in Rome.

### Step 2: In = inside a space

Explanation:
In means "in" or "inside." Use it for rooms, countries (sometimes), and enclosed places:

• in cucina = in the kitchen
• in Italia = in Italy
• nel caffè = in the café (in + il = nel)

Think: a = direction toward; in = you are inside.

Rule: Being inside → in / nel / nella

Examples:
  IT: Sono in casa.
  EN: I am at home. (inside the house)
  IT: Sofia è in cucina.
  EN: Sofia is in the kitchen.
  IT: Luca lavora nel caffè.
  EN: Luca works in the café.

### Step 3: Con = with · Per = for (purpose or recipient)

Explanation:
Con joins people: Luca è con Sofia = Luca is with Sofia.

Per explains why or for whom:

• Soldi per l'affitto = money for the rent
• Grazie per l'aiuto = thanks for the help
• Un caffè per Marco = a coffee for Marco

Rule: Con + person = "with" · Per + noun = "for"

Examples:
  IT: Luca è con Sofia.
  EN: Luca is with Sofia.
  IT: Soldi per l'affitto.
  EN: Money for the rent.
  IT: Grazie per l'aiuto.
  EN: Thanks for the help.

### Practice

Q1. Luca goes to the café. Which is correct?
     [0] Luca va in caffè.
   → [1] Luca va al caffè.
     [2] Luca va a il caffè.
   Explanation: a + il combines to al: Luca va al caffè.

Q2. "I am in the kitchen." Pick the right sentence.
     [0] Sono a cucina.
   → [1] Sono in cucina.
     [2] Sono con cucina.
   Explanation: Inside a room uses in: Sono in cucina.

Q3. "Money for the rent." Which phrase is correct?
     [0] Soldi con l'affitto.
   → [1] Soldi per l'affitto.
     [2] Soldi a l'affitto.
   Explanation: Per expresses purpose or recipient: per l'affitto = for the rent.

GRAMMAR BATCH 16–20: Time words and movement verbs
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 16-20
Title: Time words and movement verbs

Intro:
Chapters 16–20 add when things happen (oggi, domani) and how people move (andare, venire, tornare). Here is how those pieces fit together.

### Step 1: Time words usually come first

Explanation:
Italian often opens with when something happens. The verb stays in normal order after that.

• oggi = today
• domani = tomorrow
• ieri = yesterday

Oggi Luca cerca lavoro. = Today Luca looks for work.
Domani Sofia viene. = Tomorrow Sofia is coming.

Rule: Time word + subject + verb = natural Italian order

Examples:
  IT: Oggi è lunedì.
  EN: Today is Monday.
  IT: Domani Luca lavora.
  EN: Tomorrow Luca works.
  IT: Ieri piove.
  EN: Yesterday it rained.

### Step 2: Andare = to go · Venire = to come · Tornare = to return

Explanation:
These three verbs describe movement — and they conjugate differently:

• andare: vado / va → Luca va al bar. (Luca goes to the bar.)
• venire: vengo / viene → Sofia viene domani. (Sofia is coming tomorrow.)
• tornare: torno / torna → Luca torna a casa. (Luca returns home.)

Pair them with a (direction): tornare a casa, andare a Roma.

Rule: Movement verb + a + place = go/come/return to …

Examples:
  IT: Luca va al bar.
  EN: Luca goes to the bar.
  IT: Sofia viene domani.
  EN: Sofia is coming tomorrow.
  IT: Luca torna a casa.
  EN: Luca returns home.

### Step 3: Present tense = happening now or regularly

Explanation:
The story uses present tense for what is happening now and for habits:

Luca cerca lavoro. = Luca is looking for work (now / these days).
Luca lavora al bar. = Luca works at the bar (regularly).

You do not need a separate "is ...-ing" form - context tells you.

Rule: One present tense covers "does" and "is doing"

Examples:
  IT: Luca cerca lavoro.
  EN: Luca is looking for work.
  IT: Sofia parla italiano.
  EN: Sofia speaks Italian.
  IT: Marco lavora molto.
  EN: Marco works a lot.

### Practice

Q1. "Tomorrow Sofia is coming." Which is correct?
   → [0] Domani Sofia viene.
     [1] Sofia domani venire.
     [2] Domani Sofia va.
   Explanation: Time first, then subject + verb: Domani Sofia viene. Venire = to come.

Q2. Luca returns home. Pick the right sentence.
   → [0] Luca torna a casa.
     [1] Luca torna in casa a.
     [2] Luca va tornare casa.
   Explanation: Tornare a casa is the fixed pattern for "return home."

Q3. "Luca goes to the bar." Which is correct?
     [0] Luca viene al bar.
   → [1] Luca va al bar.
     [2] Luca va in bar.
   Explanation: Andare (va) = to go. Al bar = to the bar (a + il).

Batch key: 21-25
Title: Passato prossimo: completed events

Intro:
In chapters 21-25, you mainly read completed actions/events. This is mostly passato prossimo.

### Step 1: Passato prossimo = ha/è + past participle

Explanation:
Passato prossimo is used for a completed past event.

Form: helper verb (ha or è) + past participle.

In the story, some movement/change verbs use essere, such as è arrivato and è tornato. Other verbs use avere, such as ha aperto and ha detto.

For now, learn the forms you meet as complete patterns — do not treat “movement = essere” as a rule that always works.

Examples you see:
- Luca è arrivato presto e ha aperto la porta.
- Giulia ha guardato i tavoli e non ha sorriso.
- Non ha detto niente ai colleghi.

Rule: Completed past event = ha/è + past participle (learn story forms as patterns)

Examples:
  IT: Luca è arrivato presto e ha aperto la porta.
  EN: Luca arrived early and opened the door.
  IT: Giulia ha guardato i tavoli e non ha sorriso.
  EN: Giulia looked at the tables and did not smile.
  IT: Non ha detto niente ai colleghi.
  EN: He did not say anything to his colleagues.

### Step 2: Irregular participles you already saw

Explanation:
Some participles are irregular. Your job is to recognize them in the story.

In these chapters you already saw:
- aperto
- chiuso
- detto

Rule: Recognize irregular participles in passato prossimo

Examples:
  IT: Luca è arrivato presto e ha aperto la porta.

  IT: Ha chiuso la porta un momento.

  IT: Non ha detto niente ai colleghi.

Explanation:
Imperfetto describes how things were: feelings, descriptions, and ongoing situations.

GRAMMAR BATCH 31–35: Connecting ideas: se, time, and reason
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 31-35
Title: Connecting ideas: se, time, and reason

Intro:
Chapters 31-35 use connectors to link ideas: se (possible situation/result), quando/mentre/poi (time), perché (reason), and però/almeno (contrast/at least).

### Step 1: Se + present: a possible situation

Explanation:
You do not need formal terminology here. In these chapters, se works like:

"If this happens / is possible…" and then a result.

Se + present introduces a possible situation. The result can use the present or another tense depending on what you mean.

Examples:
- present result: Se viene poca gente, non importa.
- past result: Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro.

Rule: Se + present = possible situation; the result tense depends on meaning

Examples:
  IT: Se viene poca gente, non importa.
  EN: If few people come, it does not matter.
  IT: Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro.
  EN: If nobody arrives, at least we did the work.
  IT: Non possiamo solo aspettare. Dobbiamo parlare ancora, se possiamo.
  EN: We cannot only wait. We have to talk again, if we can.

### Step 2: Quando / mentre / poi: time order

Explanation:
These words help you follow the story:

- quando: "when"
- mentre: "while"
- poi: "then/after that"

Rule: Use connectors to follow time order

Examples:
  IT: Quando ha aperto la porta, Giulia era già nella sala.
  EN: When he opened the door, Giulia was already in the room.
  IT: Mentre portava i tavoli, Luca ascoltava la strada.
  EN: While he was carrying the tables, Luca listened to the street.
  IT: Poi la porta si è aperta. Marco è arrivato.
  EN: Then the door opened. Marco arrived.

### Step 3: Perché / però / almeno

Explanation:
These words add meaning after the grammar work is done:

- perché = "because/why"
- però = "but/however"
- almeno = "at least"

Rule: Reason (perché) + contrast (però) + minimum (almeno)

Examples:
  IT: L’altra parte non risponde, e io non so perché.
  EN: The other side does not answer, and I do not know why.
  IT: Sì. Però io voglio gente qui.
  EN: Yes. But I want people here.
  IT: Va bene. Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro.
  EN: Okay. If nobody arrives, at least we did the work.

GRAMMAR BATCH 36–40: Reading clearly: pronouns and who did what
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 36-40
Title: Reading clearly: pronouns and who did what

Intro:
Chapters 36-40 stay in past narration. This lesson is mainly reading strategy: track who acts, and map pronouns like gli / le to the person who receives the action. That helps Speak scenes where you retell decisions and events.

### Step 1: Passato prossimo: find who did the action

Explanation:
Passato prossimo marks a completed action with ha/è + past participle.

Ask: who is the subject of that helper verb? Usually a named person (Luca, Nonna Rosa, Giulia) in the same sentence or the previous one.

Rule: Helper + past participle = the action. Name the subject before you translate.

Examples:
  IT: Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare vicino alla porta.
  EN: Nonna Rosa called Luca and told him to stay near the door.
  IT: Marco ha portato un caffè a Nonna Rosa, e lei ha ascoltato Luca bene.
  EN: Marco brought a coffee to Nonna Rosa, and she listened to Luca well.
  IT: Luca ha sentito le parole e ha capito che poteva scegliere.
  EN: Luca heard the words and understood that he could choose.

### Step 2: Gli = to him / for him (the recipient)

Explanation:
Gli is an indirect-object pronoun: to him / for him.

Do not guess from "the nearest word." Ask: who receives the action?

In "Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto…", Nonna speaks, and Luca receives the message → gli = to Luca.

In a line like "Marco ha parlato con Luca e gli ha detto…", context still decides who "him" is — usually the person receiving the message.

Rule: gli = to him / for him → identify the recipient of the action

Examples:
  IT: Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare vicino alla porta.
  EN: Nonna Rosa called Luca and told him to stay near the door.
  IT: Sofia ha parlato a Luca e gli ha chiesto cosa vuole.
  EN: Sofia spoke to Luca and asked him what he wants.

### Step 3: Le = to her / for her (same idea)

Explanation:
Le (indirect object) = to her / for her. Same strategy: find the recipient.

Do not confuse this le with the article le ("the" before feminine plural nouns), as in Le persone… (= The people…).

Rule: le (pronoun) = to her → find who receives the action

Examples:
  IT: Luca ha chiamato Sofia e le ha detto la verità.
  EN: Luca called Sofia and told her the truth.
  IT: Giulia ha parlato a Sofia e le ha spiegato l’orario.
  EN: Giulia spoke to Sofia and explained the schedule to her.

### Step 4: Useful for Speak 27–40: forse, se, per adesso

Explanation:
These chapters ask you to produce more than single facts:

• forse + present = possibility now/soon (Il padrone forse vende…)
• se + present = if this happens, this result happens
• per adesso = for now (a temporary choice, not forever)

You already met se in the connectors lesson. Here you use them to explain plans and decisions.

Rule: forse / se / per adesso help you talk about possibility and temporary choices

Examples:
  IT: Il padrone forse vende il caffè. Non ha deciso.
  EN: The owner might sell the café. He has not decided.
  IT: Se la gente viene, il padrone vede lavoro.
  EN: If people come, the owner sees work.
  IT: Per adesso resto a Roma.
  EN: For now I stay in Rome.

### Practice

Q1. In "Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare.", what does "gli" refer to?
   → [0] Luca (the person who receives the message)
     [1] Nonna Rosa
     [2] la porta
   Explanation: gli = to him. Luca receives what Nonna said.

Q2. In "Luca ha chiamato Sofia e le ha detto la verità.", what does "le" mean?
   → [0] to her (Sofia)
     [1] the people
     [2] the truth
   Explanation: Here le is the pronoun "to her," not the article "the."

Q3. What does "Per adesso resto a Roma" communicate?
   → [0] A temporary choice to stay
     [1] A permanent promise never to leave
     [2] That Rome sold the café
   Explanation: Per adesso = for now — the future can still change.
