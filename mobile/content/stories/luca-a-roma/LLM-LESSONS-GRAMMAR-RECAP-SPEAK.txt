GRAMMAR BATCH 1–5: Essere and avere
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 1-5
Title: Essere and avere

Intro:
Chapters 1–5 use two foundation verbs: essere (to be) and avere (to have). Here is how Italian builds sentences with them, and why Italian speakers often leave out words like "I" and "you".

### Step 1: Essere: Who someone is, where they are, or how they feel

Explanation:
What it means:
• sono = I am
• sei = you are (talking to a friend)
• è = he is / she is / it is

The pattern:
In English, you always have to say words like "I", "you", or "he". In Italian, the person words are:
• io = I
• tu = you
• lui = he
• lei = she
• noi = we
• voi = you all
• loro = they

Because the verb ending already tells us who is speaking (sono = I am, sei = you are), Italian speakers usually drop words like io and tu.

When you see è after a person’s name, read it as "is": Sofia è al bar = Sofia is at the café.

Rule: sono = "I am" · [Name] + è = "[Name] is"

Examples:
  IT: Luca è a Roma.
  EN: Luca is in Rome.
  IT: Sofia è stanca.
  EN: Sofia is tired.
  IT: È mattina.
  EN: It is morning.

### Step 2: Avere: What someone has — including feelings like hunger

Explanation:
What it means:
• ho = I have
• hai = you have
• ha = he has / she has

The pattern:
Avere means "to have." Italian uses it for possessions, but also for physical feelings that English expresses with "to be":
• ho fame = I am hungry (literally: "I have hunger")
• ho sete = I am thirsty (literally: "I have thirst")
• ho sonno = I am sleepy (literally: "I have sleepiness")

Just like with essere, you do not need to say io: Ho fame already tells us who is hungry.

Rule: Ho / ha + feeling = "I am / he is [hungry / thirsty / sleepy]"

Examples:
  IT: Ho fame.
  EN: I am hungry. (literally: I have hunger)
  IT: Luca ha pochi soldi.
  EN: Luca has little money.
  IT: Non ho molti soldi.
  EN: I do not have much money.

### Step 3: Non: Making sentences negative

Explanation:
What it means:
• non = not / do not

The pattern:
To make any Italian sentence negative, put non immediately in front of the verb. Nothing else moves:
• Ho fame. (I am hungry.) → Non ho fame. (I am not hungry.)
• Luca è a casa. (Luca is at home.) → Luca non è a casa. (Luca is not at home.)

Rule: non + verb = negative sentence

Examples:
  IT: Non ho fame.
  EN: I am not hungry.
  IT: Luca non è a casa.
  EN: Luca is not at home.
  IT: Non è tardi.
  EN: It is not late.

### Practice

Q1. Luca is tired. Which sentence is correct?
   → [0] Luca è stanco.
     [1] Luca ha stanco.
     [2] Luca sono stanco.
   Explanation: Use essere (è) for "is" + a description: Luca è stanco. Avere (ha) means "has."

Q2. How do you say "I am hungry" in Italian?
   → [0] Ho fame.
     [1] Sono fame.
     [2] Ho affamato.
   Explanation: Italian uses avere (to have) for feelings: Ho fame (literally "I have hunger").

Q3. Which sentence means "Luca does not have much money"?
   → [0] Luca non ha molti soldi.
     [1] Luca ha non molti soldi.
     [2] Non Luca ha molti soldi.
   Explanation: Put non directly in front of the verb ha: Luca non ha molti soldi.

GRAMMAR BATCH 6–10: Volere and cercare
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 6-10
Title: Volere and cercare

Intro:
As Luca arrives in Rome, he needs to express what he wants and search for work. Two verbs drive these sentences: volere (to want) and cercare (to look for).

### Step 1: Volere: Saying what you want to do

Explanation:
What it means:
• voglio = I want
• vuoi = you want
• vuole = he / she wants

The pattern:
To say what you want to do, pair volere with a basic action word (ending in -are, -ere, or -ire):
• Voglio mangiare. = I want to eat.
• Luca vuole restare. = Luca wants to stay.

You can also want an item:
• Voglio un caffè. = I want a coffee.

Rule: voglio / vuole + action word = "want to [do something]"

Examples:
  IT: Voglio mangiare.
  EN: I want to eat.
  IT: Luca vuole un lavoro.
  EN: Luca wants a job.
  IT: Vuole restare a Roma.
  EN: He wants to stay in Rome.

### Step 2: Cercare: Looking for something or someone

Explanation:
What it means:
• cerco = I am looking for / I look for
• cerchi = you are looking for
• cerca = he / she is looking for

The pattern:
In Italian, the verb cercare already includes the meaning of "for"! You do not add any extra word:
• Cerco un lavoro. = I am looking for a job.
• Luca cerca Sofia. = Luca is looking for Sofia.

Rule: cerco / cerca + item = "I am / he is looking for [item]"

Examples:
  IT: Luca cerca un lavoro.
  EN: Luca is looking for a job.
  IT: Cerchi un lavoro?
  EN: Are you looking for a job?
  IT: Scusa, cerco un lavoro.
  EN: Excuse me, I am looking for a job.

### Step 3: Scusa and per favore: Polite requests

Explanation:
What it means:
• Scusa = Excuse me (friendly, spoken to one person)
• Per favore = Please

The pattern:
Use Scusa at the beginning of a question when approaching someone for help.
Use Per favore at the end of an order or request.

Rule: Scusa + question = polite opening · Per favore = please

Examples:
  IT: Scusa, dov'è il bar?
  EN: Excuse me, where is the café?
  IT: Un caffè, per favore.
  EN: A coffee, please.
  IT: Scusa, cerco un lavoro.
  EN: Excuse me, I am looking for a job.

### Practice

Q1. "I want to eat." Which sentence is correct?
   → [0] Voglio mangiare.
     [1] Voglio mangio.
     [2] Mangio voglio.
   Explanation: Pair voglio with the basic action word mangiare: Voglio mangiare.

Q2. Luca is looking for a job. Pick the right sentence.
   → [0] Luca cerca un lavoro.
     [1] Luca cerco un lavoro.
     [2] Luca cerca per un lavoro.
   Explanation: Use cerca for he/she. Cercare already includes "for," so no per is needed.

Q3. Which sentence politely starts a question to someone on the street?
   → [0] Scusa, dov'è il bar?
     [1] Dov'è scusa il bar?
     [2] Scusa il bar dov'è?
   Explanation: Start politely with Scusa at the beginning: Scusa, dov'è il bar?

GRAMMAR BATCH 11–15: Prepositions: a, in, con, per
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 11-15
Title: Prepositions: a, in, con, per

Intro:
Small connecting words tell you direction, location, company, and purpose. Here is how four essential Italian prepositions work.

### Step 1: A: Going to or staying in a city or at home

Explanation:
What it means:
• a = to / in / at

The pattern:
Italian uses a before city names and the word casa (home):
• Luca va a Roma. = Luca goes to Rome. (movement)
• Marco è a Roma. = Marco is in Rome. (location)
• a casa = at home / (going) home

When a combines with il (the), it blends into al:
• a + il bar = al bar (to the café / at the café)

Rule: a + city/casa = "to/in/at" · a + il = al

Examples:
  IT: Luca va a Roma.
  EN: Luca goes to Rome.
  IT: Vado al caffè.
  EN: I am going to the café.
  IT: Marco è a Roma.
  EN: Marco is in Rome.

### Step 2: In: Being inside a room, building, or country

Explanation:
What it means:
• in = in / inside

The pattern:
Use in when you are inside a room or a country:
• in cucina = in the kitchen
• in Italia = in Italy

When in combines with il (the), it blends into nel:
• in + il caffè = nel caffè (inside the café)

Rule: in + room/country = inside that space · in + il = nel

Examples:
  IT: Sono in casa.
  EN: I am at home. (inside the house)
  IT: Sofia è in cucina.
  EN: Sofia is in the kitchen.
  IT: Luca lavora nel caffè.
  EN: Luca works in the café.

### Step 3: Con (with) and Per (for)

Explanation:
What it means:
• con = with
• per = for

The pattern:
Use con to connect people:
• Luca è con Sofia. = Luca is with Sofia.

Use per to explain purpose, a recipient, or reasons:
• soldi per l'affitto = money for the rent
• grazie per l'aiuto = thanks for the help
• un caffè per Marco = a coffee for Marco

Rule: con + person = "with" · per + noun = "for"

Examples:
  IT: Luca è con Sofia.
  EN: Luca is with Sofia.
  IT: Soldi per l'affitto.
  EN: Money for the rent.
  IT: Grazie per l'aiuto.
  EN: Thanks for the help.

### Practice

Q1. Luca goes to the café. Which sentence is correct?
   → [0] Luca va al caffè.
     [1] Luca va in caffè.
     [2] Luca va a il caffè.
   Explanation: a + il blends smoothly into al: Luca va al caffè.

Q2. "I am in the kitchen." Pick the right sentence.
   → [0] Sono in cucina.
     [1] Sono a cucina.
     [2] Sono con cucina.
   Explanation: Use in for rooms: Sono in cucina.

Q3. "Money for the rent." Which phrase is correct?
   → [0] Soldi per l'affitto.
     [1] Soldi con l'affitto.
     [2] Soldi a l'affitto.
   Explanation: Per expresses purpose or recipient: per l'affitto = for the rent.

GRAMMAR BATCH 16–20: Time words and movement verbs
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 16-20
Title: Time words and movement verbs

Intro:
Chapters 16–20 add when events take place (oggi, domani) and how characters move around Rome (andare, venire, tornare). Here is how they work.

### Step 1: Time words: Setting when things happen

Explanation:
What it means:
• oggi = today
• domani = tomorrow
• ieri = yesterday

The pattern:
In Italian, time words usually come right at the start of the sentence. The rest of the sentence follows in normal order:
• Oggi Luca cerca lavoro. = Today Luca looks for work.
• Domani Sofia viene. = Tomorrow Sofia is coming.

Rule: [Time word] + [person] + [verb] = natural Italian sentence order

Examples:
  IT: Oggi è lunedì.
  EN: Today is Monday.
  IT: Domani Luca lavora.
  EN: Tomorrow Luca works.
  IT: Ieri era tranquillo.
  EN: Yesterday it was quiet.

### Step 2: Three key movement verbs: Andare, Venire, Tornare

Explanation:
What it means:
• andare (to go): vado = I go, va = he / she goes
• venire (to come): vengo = I come, viene = he / she comes
• tornare (to return): torno = I return, torna = he / she returns

The pattern:
Movement verbs naturally pair with a (direction):
• andare a Roma = to go to Rome
• tornare a casa = to return home
• venire al bar = to come to the café

Rule: Movement verb + a + destination = go / come / return to a place

Examples:
  IT: Luca va al bar.
  EN: Luca goes to the café.
  IT: Sofia viene domani.
  EN: Sofia is coming tomorrow.
  IT: Luca torna a casa.
  EN: Luca returns home.

### Step 3: Present tense: Happening now or happening regularly

Explanation:
What it means:
In Italian, one present tense form covers both "does" and "is doing".

The pattern:
You do not need a separate "-ing" helper word like in English:
• Luca cerca lavoro. = Luca looks for work OR Luca is looking for work.
• Luca lavora al bar. = Luca works at the café OR Luca is working at the café.

The context of the sentence makes the meaning clear.

Rule: One present tense form covers "does" and "is doing"

Examples:
  IT: Luca cerca lavoro.
  EN: Luca is looking for work.
  IT: Sofia parla italiano.
  EN: Sofia speaks Italian.
  IT: Marco lavora molto.
  EN: Marco works a lot.

### Practice

Q1. "Tomorrow Sofia is coming." Which sentence is correct?
   → [0] Domani Sofia viene.
     [1] Sofia domani venire.
     [2] Domani Sofia va.
   Explanation: Put the time word first, then person + verb: Domani Sofia viene. Venire = to come.

Q2. Luca returns home. Pick the right sentence.
   → [0] Luca torna a casa.
     [1] Luca torna in casa a.
     [2] Luca va tornare casa.
   Explanation: Tornare a casa is the natural Italian phrase for returning home.

Q3. "Luca goes to the café." Which sentence is correct?
   → [0] Luca va al bar.
     [1] Luca viene al bar.
     [2] Luca va in bar.
   Explanation: Andare (va) means "goes." Al bar means "to the café" (a + il).

GRAMMAR BATCH 21–25: Passato prossimo: completed events
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 21-25
Title: Passato prossimo: completed events

Intro:
In Chapters 21–25, the story begins recounting completed past actions (events that happened and finished). Italian uses the passato prossimo for this.

### Step 1: The two-word team for past actions

Explanation:
What it means:
Passato prossimo is the past tense for specific events that happened (like "Luca arrived", "he opened the door").

The pattern:
In Italian, you build this past tense using a team of two words:
• Word 1 (Helper word): ha (has) or è (is)
• Word 2 (Past action word): the past form of the verb

For regular action words ending in -are, the past form ends in -ato:
• guardare (to look) → ha guardato (looked)
• chiamare (to call) → ha chiamato (called)
• arrivare (to arrive) → è arrivato (arrived)
• tornare (to return) → è tornato (returned)

Notice that some verbs take ha and others take è. Learn them as natural pairs from the story!

Rule: ha / è + past action word = completed past event

Examples:
  IT: Luca è arrivato presto e ha aperto la porta.
  EN: Luca arrived early and opened the door.
  IT: Giulia ha guardato i tavoli e non ha sorriso.
  EN: Giulia looked at the tables and did not smile.
  IT: Non ha detto niente ai colleghi.
  EN: He did not say anything to his colleagues.

### Step 2: Common irregular past action words

Explanation:
What it means:
Some verbs have special short past forms that do not end in -ato.

The pattern:
Here are three common ones you saw in these chapters:
• aprire (to open) → aperto (ha aperto = opened)
• chiudere (to close) → chiuso (ha chiuso = closed)
• dire (to say) → detto (ha detto = said)

When you see these words after ha, you know an action was completed.

Rule: Recognize aperto (opened), chiuso (closed), and detto (said)

Examples:
  IT: Luca è arrivato presto e ha aperto la porta.
  EN: Luca arrived early and opened the door.
  IT: Ha chiuso la porta un momento.
  EN: He closed the door for a moment.
  IT: Non ha detto niente ai colleghi.
  EN: He did not say anything to his colleagues.

### Practice

Q1. In "Luca è arrivato presto", which words show the past tense?
   → [0] è arrivato
     [1] Luca
     [2] presto
   Explanation: The two-word team è (helper) + arrivato (past form) creates the past tense.

Q2. In the story, which form correctly says: "Luca ___ to the café early"?
   → [0] è tornato
     [1] ha tornato
     [2] torna
   Explanation: Tornare pairs with è in the past: Luca è tornato presto.

Q3. In "Non ha detto niente ai colleghi", which word is the past action word?
   → [0] detto
     [1] ha
     [2] niente
   Explanation: Detto is the past form of dire (to say): ha detto = he said.

GRAMMAR BATCH 26–30: Imperfetto vs passato prossimo: background vs events
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 26-30
Title: Imperfetto vs passato prossimo: background vs events

Intro:
Good storytelling mixes two past tenses: l’imperfetto to set the background scene, and il passato prossimo to tell the specific events that occurred.

### Step 1: Imperfetto: The background of the scene

Explanation:
What it means:
L’imperfetto helps you show what was happening or what things were like.
Think of it as the background scenery and atmosphere of the scene:
• Era sera. → It was evening.
• Pioveva. → It was raining.

The pattern:
Look for these common background forms in the story:
• era = he / she / it was (Giulia era al caffè = Giulia was at the café)
• c'erano = there were (c'erano pochi clienti = there were few customers)
• sembrava = seemed (Marco non sembrava tranquillo = Marco did not seem calm)
• sorrideva = was smiling / used to smile

Rule: Imperfetto = setting the background scene and ongoing atmosphere

Examples:
  IT: C'erano ancora pochi clienti questa mattina, come ieri.
  EN: There were still a few customers this morning, like yesterday.
  IT: Giulia era già al caffè e non sorrideva.
  EN: Giulia was already at the café and was not smiling.
  IT: Marco non sembrava tranquillo.
  EN: Marco did not seem calm.

### Step 2: Passato prossimo: The action that happens

Explanation:
What it means:
Il passato prossimo tells the sharp, completed actions that move the story forward.

The pattern:
While the background was ongoing (imperfetto), a specific action took place (passato prossimo):
• Background: Giulia era al caffè... (Giulia was at the café...)
• Event: ...Luca è arrivato. (...Luca arrived.)
• Event: Ha chiuso la porta. (He closed the door.)
• Event: Il padrone ha chiamato Luca. (The owner called Luca.)

Rule: Background (imperfetto) + Event (passato prossimo) = storytelling

Examples:
  IT: Martedì mattina Luca è tornato al caffè presto.
  EN: On Tuesday morning Luca returned to the café early.
  IT: Ha chiuso la porta un momento.
  EN: He closed the door for a moment.
  IT: Il padrone ha chiamato Luca e Giulia vicino ai tavoli.
  EN: The owner called Luca and Giulia near the tables.

### Practice

Q1. In "Giulia era già al caffè e non sorrideva", why is "era" used?
   → [0] It sets the background scene (she was already there).
     [1] It is a sudden completed action.
     [2] It is happening right now in the present.
   Explanation: Era is imperfetto. It describes the ongoing background state.

Q2. "Martedì mattina Luca ___ al caffè presto." Which form best marks the completed event?
   → [0] è tornato
     [1] era tornato
     [2] torna
   Explanation: È tornato (passato prossimo) marks the specific completed event.

Q3. In storytelling, which tense gives the "background atmosphere"?
   → [0] Imperfetto
     [1] Passato prossimo
     [2] Present tense
   Explanation: L’imperfetto paints the ongoing background atmosphere.

GRAMMAR BATCH 31–35: Connecting ideas: se, time, and reason
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 31-35
Title: Connecting ideas: se, time, and reason

Intro:
Chapters 31–35 use connectors to link ideas together: se (if), time words (quando, mentre, poi), and reason words (perché, però, almeno).

### Step 1: Se: Exploring possible situations (If...)

Explanation:
What it means:
• se = if

The pattern:
Use se to set up a condition or possibility, followed by the outcome:
• Se viene poca gente, non importa. = If few people come, it does not matter.
• Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro. = If nobody arrives, at least we did the work.

Rule: Se + condition = "If [this happens], [this is the result]"

Examples:
  IT: Se viene poca gente, non importa.
  EN: If few people come, it does not matter.
  IT: Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro.
  EN: If nobody arrives, at least we did the work.
  IT: Non possiamo solo aspettare. Dobbiamo parlare ancora, se possiamo.
  EN: We cannot only wait. We have to talk again, if we can.

### Step 2: Quando, mentre, poi: Tracking time order

Explanation:
What it means:
• quando = when
• mentre = while (two actions happening at the same time)
• poi = then / after that

The pattern:
These words show the sequence of events in a story:
• Quando ha aperto la porta... = When he opened the door...
• Mentre portava i tavoli, Luca ascoltava... = While he was carrying the tables, Luca listened...
• Poi la porta si è aperta. = Then the door opened.

Rule: quando (when) · mentre (while) · poi (then)

Examples:
  IT: Quando ha aperto la porta, Giulia era già nella sala.
  EN: When he opened the door, Giulia was already in the room.
  IT: Mentre portava i tavoli, Luca ascoltava la strada.
  EN: While he was carrying the tables, Luca listened to the street.
  IT: Poi la porta si è aperta. Marco è arrivato.
  EN: Then the door opened. Marco arrived.

### Step 3: Perché, però, almeno: Reasons and contrasts

Explanation:
What it means:
• perché = because / why
• però = but / however
• almeno = at least

The pattern:
Use these three words to give nuance to thoughts and opinions:
• perché: gives the reason (Non so perché = I do not know why)
• però: introduces a contrast (Sì. Però voglio gente qui. = Yes. But I want people here.)
• almeno: sets a positive minimum (Almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro. = At least we did the work.)

Rule: perché (because/why) · però (but) · almeno (at least)

Examples:
  IT: L’altra parte non risponde, e io non so perché.
  EN: The other side does not answer, and I do not know why.
  IT: Sì. Però io voglio gente qui.
  EN: Yes. But I want people here.
  IT: Va bene. Se non arriva nessuno, almeno abbiamo fatto il lavoro.
  EN: Okay. If nobody arrives, at least we did the work.

### Practice

Q1. "___ viene poca gente, non importa." Which word means "If"?
   → [0] Se
     [1] Quando
     [2] Poi
   Explanation: Se introduces a possibility: Se viene poca gente = If few people come.

Q2. "___ la porta si è aperta, Marco è arrivato." Which word means "Then / After that"?
   → [0] Poi
     [1] Mentre
     [2] Perché
   Explanation: Poi means "then" or "after that."

Q3. In "Non so ___.", which word completes the meaning of "why"?
   → [0] perché
     [1] però
     [2] almeno
   Explanation: Perché means both "because" and "why."

GRAMMAR BATCH 36–40: Reading clearly: pronouns and who did what
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Batch key: 36-40
Title: Reading clearly: pronouns and who did what

Intro:
As story plots grow more complex, you need to track who did what to whom. Italian uses small pronouns like gli (to him) and le (to her) to name the recipient of an action.

### Step 1: Finding who did the action in the past

Explanation:
What it means:
When you see helper + past action (ha chiamato, ha detto), look for the person who performed it.

The pattern:
The subject is often named right before the verb or in the previous clause:
• Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca... → Nonna Rosa is the one calling.
• ...e lei ha ascoltato Luca bene. → Lei (she = Nonna Rosa) is the one listening.

Rule: Helper + past action = identify the subject before translating

Examples:
  IT: Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare vicino alla porta.
  EN: Nonna Rosa called Luca and told him to stay near the door.
  IT: Marco ha portato un caffè a Nonna Rosa, e lei ha ascoltato Luca bene.
  EN: Marco brought a coffee to Nonna Rosa, and she listened to Luca well.
  IT: Luca ha sentito le parole e ha capito che poteva scegliere.
  EN: Luca heard the words and understood that he could choose.

### Step 2: Gli: To him / for him

Explanation:
What it means:
• gli = to him / for him

The pattern:
gli sits right before the verb and tells you who receives the action or message:
• Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto...
Nonna speaks → Luca receives the words → gli = to Luca (to him).

Rule: gli = "to him / for him" (the male recipient)

Examples:
  IT: Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare vicino alla porta.
  EN: Nonna Rosa called Luca and told him to stay near the door.
  IT: Sofia ha parlato a Luca e gli ha chiesto cosa vuole.
  EN: Sofia spoke to Luca and asked him what he wants.

### Step 3: Le: To her / for her

Explanation:
What it means:
• le = to her / for her

The pattern:
Just like gli is "to him", le means "to her":
• Luca ha chiamato Sofia e le ha detto la verità.
Luca speaks → Sofia receives the truth → le = to Sofia (to her).

Quick tip: Do not confuse pronoun le (to her) with the plural word le (the, as in le persone = the people).

Rule: le = "to her / for her" (the female recipient)

Examples:
  IT: Luca ha chiamato Sofia e le ha detto la verità.
  EN: Luca called Sofia and told her the truth.
  IT: Giulia ha parlato a Sofia e le ha spiegato l’orario.
  EN: Giulia spoke to Sofia and explained the schedule to her.

### Step 4: Words for plans: forse, se, per adesso

Explanation:
What it means:
• forse = maybe / perhaps
• se = if
• per adesso = for now / for the time being

The pattern:
These words help characters talk about uncertain decisions and temporary plans:
• Il padrone forse vende il caffè. = The owner might sell the café.
• Per adesso resto a Roma. = For now I stay in Rome.

Rule: forse (maybe) · se (if) · per adesso (for now)

Examples:
  IT: Il padrone forse vende il caffè. Non ha deciso.
  EN: The owner might sell the café. He has not decided.
  IT: Se la gente viene, il padrone vede lavoro.
  EN: If people come, the owner sees work.
  IT: Per adesso resto a Roma.
  EN: For now I stay in Rome.

### Practice

Q1. In "Nonna Rosa ha chiamato Luca e gli ha detto di restare", what does "gli" mean?
   → [0] to him (Luca, the person receiving the message)
     [1] Nonna Rosa
     [2] the door
   Explanation: gli means "to him." Luca receives what Nonna Rosa said.

Q2. In "Luca ha chiamato Sofia e le ha detto la verità", what does "le" mean?
   → [0] to her (Sofia)
     [1] the people
     [2] the truth
   Explanation: Here le is the pronoun "to her." Sofia receives the truth.

Q3. What does "Per adesso resto a Roma" communicate?
   → [0] A temporary choice to stay for now
     [1] A permanent promise never to leave
     [2] That Rome sold the café
   Explanation: Per adesso means "for now" — the future can still change.
